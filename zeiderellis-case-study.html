Case Study · Mythos Systems

Zeiderelli’s Pizza & Subs

A working pizza shop at 16 S Market St, Duncannon, PA got the full stack — website, point-of-sale, control panel, and terminal hardware — built by one person working the counter.

The Situation

A good shop, expensive pipes

The shop’s food was fine. Its pipes were not: online orders flowed through third parties charging 25–30% over menu price, the point-of-sale was a vendor stack with its own fees, and the shop had no website of its own. Every order paid rent to somebody else.

Delivered · Website

zeiderellis.dpdns.org

Full menu site with a weekly lunch-special rhythm, Facebook and Maps integration, mailing-list capture into Google Sheets, and Restaurant structured data that passed Google’s Rich Results test with zero errors.

Visit the live site →

Delivered · Point of Sale

Fees designed out

A DIY POS in Python, Flask, and SQLite: tap-to-add ordering from the real menu, order queue, history, and daily totals. Cash completes at $0 fee. Cards run through Stripe-hosted checkout — the card never touches the shop’s server.

Delivered · Control Panel

The owner’s hands on the wheel

Menu and prices editable in the panel — which became the source of truth the printed menu is reprinted from. Weekly specials, availability toggles, employee clock in/out with CSV export, a capped card-fee surcharge toggle, and a printer registry with test prints.

Delivered · Hardware

A 2015 all-in-one, reborn

The shop’s Acer Aspire all-in-one became the POS terminal: Debian 13 minimal, a browser kiosk that boots straight to the register, and a print relay driving the shop’s existing Epson receipt printers and cash drawer. Original Windows drive untouched.

Verification

496 checks, all passing

The system ships with an automated test suite covering the POS, the print queue, the hosting layer, and the control panel — plus live verification of every redirect, the structured data, and the mobile layout. Honest limits, stated in the owner’s own overview document: live Stripe keys, the real printer hardware test, and go-live remain the shop’s moves.
